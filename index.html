How the early warning works

Five connected parts help you decide where to spend your time. Nothing here replaces your professional judgement.

1

Signals

Attendance, assignment completion and marks from three tests. Marks are weighted towards the most recent test, so recent change matters most.

2

Risk indicator

A 0–100 score built from fixed weights plus two transparent trend penalties. Every point is shown — no model, no mystery.

3

Supporting factors

The signals behind the score, written in plain English and ranked by impact, so you can see why a student appears here.

4

Interventions

Two to four concrete next steps matched to the reasons, each with a priority. Tick them off and add a session note as you go.

5

Risk forecast

A small logistic regression estimates the chance a student becomes High risk by the next test. It is trained on 300 synthetic past students from a noisy hidden process, separate from the rule score.

The score

0.35 × attendance + 0.25 × assignments + 0.40 × weighted test average

Weighted test average: T1 20%, T2 30%, T3 50%. Penalties: −5 if marks fell in each of the last three tests, −5 if attendance dropped 10+ points over four weeks. 70+ on track · 50–69 watch · below 50 needs support.

Forecast, not a verdict

Rule score = now · Forecast = next test

Holdout accuracy: calculating · trained on synthetic data. The forecast never changes the rule score.

Use this as a conversation starter, not a verdict. Context such as health, bereavement, language or home circumstances belongs in the room with you — the data cannot see it.
